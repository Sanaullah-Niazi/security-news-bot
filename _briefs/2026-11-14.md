### International Police Take Down Malware Infrastructure in Operation Endgame

An international law enforcement action known as Operation Endgame has disrupted infrastructure behind Gootloader, CastleRAT, and StealC malware. Authorities seized hundreds of servers and domains while recovering approximately 6.4 million stolen victim credentials. Anyone using impacted systems or reusing passwords could be at risk. Users should reset their passwords and enable multi-factor authentication across important accounts.

### Check Point Security Gateways Targeted in Active Zero-Day Attacks

Attackers are actively exploiting a high-severity zero-day vulnerability in Check Point Security Gateways to gain root access and deploy data-wiping malware on connected hosts. The flaw has reportedly been targeted since late October and was added to CISA's catalog of actively exploited bugs. Organizations utilizing affected Check Point products are at risk of network compromise. Administrators should apply the newly released emergency hotfixes immediately.

### Hardware Implants Discovered in Resold Trezor Crypto Wallets

Cryptocurrency wallet manufacturer Trezor confirmed that three Safe 5 devices sold by a Southeast Asian reseller were modified with malicious hardware implants. The rogue circuit boards were designed to capture recovery phrases directly from the device screen and transmit them over cellular networks. Anyone who purchased hardware wallets through unauthorized third parties may be exposed to fund theft. Crypto holders should buy hardware wallets exclusively from official vendors and inspect new devices carefully.

<span class="brief-src">Sources: [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/trezor-confirms-crypto-stealing-implants-in-three-wallets-releases-fix/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/trezor-confirms-hidden-hardware.html)</span>

### FBI Warns of $400 Million Crypto Theft Driven by Deepfake Video Calls

The FBI reported that North Korean threat actors have stolen over $400 million in cryptocurrency using deepfake video calls as part of a social engineering campaign. Attackers used manipulated video to deceive victims during live interactions and gain unauthorized access to funds. Anyone handling digital assets or high-value accounts could be targeted by similar impersonation schemes. Users should verify unexpected video calls through separate trusted communication channels before sharing sensitive information.

<span class="brief-src">Source: [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/fbi-says-north-korean-hackers-stole.html)</span>

### SilentAir Cyberespionage Campaign Intercepts Mobile Calls and SMS

A cyberespionage campaign dubbed SilentAir compromised mobile network nodes across Asia to intercept telephone calls and text messages. According to security researchers, the threat actors specifically targeted Taiwanese subscribers to steal two-factor authentication codes delivered via SMS. Users relying on text-based verification on affected networks face potential account compromise. Individuals should migrate from SMS-based two-factor authentication to authenticator apps or hardware keys.

<span class="brief-src">Sources: [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/silentair-hackers-intercept-calls-sms-from-inside-mobile-networks/), [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/silentair-hijacks-asian-telecom.html)</span>

## What to do this week

- Reset passwords on critical accounts and switch from SMS text messages to authenticator apps or physical hardware keys for two-factor authentication.
- Apply emergency security patches immediately if you manage Check Point network gateway infrastructure.
- Purchase hardware cryptocurrency wallets directly from official manufacturer sites rather than third-party resellers.
- Verify the identity of unknown contacts on video calls via an independent channel before sharing credentials or approving transfers.
